## Day 14 - Ai Insights
**Post from:** PERSONAL PROFILE
**Topic:** What large language models can and cannot do with structured data
**Characters:** 1486

```
LLMs are not data validators. They are language models.

This distinction matters more than most people realise when they start exploring AI tools for data quality work.

Large language models are genuinely impressive at interpretation, summarisation, and generating structured outputs from unstructured text. Ask one to explain a trend, draft a summary, or classify a piece of free-form input - it performs well.

Ask it to validate 50,000 rows of supplier invoice data against domain-specific formatting rules, and the picture changes fast.

LLMs hallucinate missing values rather than flagging them. They miss systematic duplicates that a rule-based check catches immediately. They apply logic inconsistently across rows because they are predicting tokens, not executing deterministic validation logic.

For structured tabular data, domain-aware rule engines outperform general-purpose AI every time. Not because AI is bad - but because the task requires precision and repeatability, not probabilistic language generation.

The right division of labour: use structured validation to clean and flag the data, then use LLMs to help interpret what those flags mean in context.

ColtraDataAi sits on the validation side of that line. Eight domain-specific cleaners, rule-based logic, consistent outputs. No hallucinated corrections.

See how domain-aware validation differs from AI summarisation at app.coltradata.com.
```
