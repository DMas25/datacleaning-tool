The cost of a data quality problem is rarely the cost of fixing it.

It is the cost of the decision made on it before anyone noticed.

A supplier record with a missing VAT number processed through month-end. A logistics dataset with duplicate consignment references that quietly inflated the shipment count. An inventory file with inconsistent unit measures that triggered a reorder that did not need to happen.

None of these are dramatic failures. They are ordinary ones. And they are expensive in ways that do not show up as a single line item - they show up as time, corrections, wasted resource, and lost confidence in the numbers.

Here is what makes this worse: most organisations discover data quality problems at the worst possible moment. Not before the data enters a workflow. After it has already informed a report, a budget, a decision.

Catching errors upstream - before the data reaches anything - costs a fraction of what it costs to unpick them downstream.
