Every business decision you only as good as the data underneath it.

Every forecast. Every budget. Every operational change. Every investment case. Each one traces back to a number, a record, a file that someone compiled and passed along. If that source data is inconsistent, duplicated, or incorrectly formatted, the decision built on it is unstable - regardless of how sophisticated the analysis on top looks.

The problem is not that businesses ignore data quality. It is that they treat it as a downstream concern. Something to investigate when a report throws up an anomaly. Something to clean up after the fact.

By then, decisions have already been made.

Verify the foundation before you build on it. The same logic applies to your data before it feeds a model, a dashboard, a budget, or a board presentation.

The businesses that consistently make better decisions are not always the ones with the best analysts. They are often the ones who sorted out their data quality first.
